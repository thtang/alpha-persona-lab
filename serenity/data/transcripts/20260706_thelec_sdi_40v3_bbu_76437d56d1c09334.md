 Samsung SDI to Start Mass Production of BBU Battery Cells in Malaysia in July
 New 21700 "40V3" Cell Targets AI Data Centers With Tabless Design and Dual Safety Structure

 Samsung SDI will begin commercial production of a new battery cell for battery backup units (BBUs), a critical component in AI data centers, at its Malaysia plant in July. The decision to use Malaysia as the production base reflects strong demand in the U.S. for non-China battery supply chains.
 According to industry sources on April 23, Samsung SDI will mass-produce its newly unveiled 21700 cylindrical battery cell, dubbed 40V3, for BBU applications at the Malaysian facility.
 The company is also understood to be in discussions with global big tech firms, including Amazon, Meta and Google, as demand for BBU batteries surges alongside expanding investments in AI data centers.
 Data centers require immediate backup power in the event of outages or fluctuations. With data center investments rising, particularly in the U.S., orders for power equipment and energy storage systems (ESS) have increased, alongside rapid deployment of rack-level backup systems such as BBUs.
 Samsung SDI sees strong growth potential in the BBU battery market, projecting an average annual growth rate of about 14% from 2025 to 2030, according to its fourth-quarter earnings call last year.
 A company official said it plans to expand its presence with BBU-specific cells featuring tabless technology to enhance high-power performance. The 40V3 incorporates this design, which removes current-collecting tabs and allows current to flow along the full length of the electrodes, reducing internal resistance and heat generation.
 The 40V3 is designed to replace the existing 18650 cylindrical cells used in BBUs. By adopting the larger 21700 form factor, it meets customer requirements for longer backup duration, higher output and improved thermal safety. A key feature is its dual safety structure, which adds a bottom vent to the conventional top gas release mechanism. The cell also increases nickel content to 91% to boost energy density.
 Batteries used in data centers are categorized by function. BBU batteries are installed within server racks to supply power to individual units in a distributed architecture. In contrast, uninterruptible power supply (UPS) batteries operate in a centralized configuration, delivering power to entire facilities from separate locations. Both require high output and safety for immediate response in emergencies. Containerized large-scale ESS units, installed externally, store and supply power to support overall data center operations.
 Samsung SDI differentiates battery form factors by application. It supplies cylindrical batteries for BBUs and prismatic batteries for UPS systems. Its UPS products are based on lithium manganese oxide (LMO), achieving high output characteristics. For containerized ESS, the company offers the Samsung Battery Box, which integrates batteries with safety and thermal management systems.
 Rival LG Energy Solution uses cylindrical batteries for BBUs and pouch-type batteries for UPS systems, while also applying pouch cells in grid-scale ESS and preparing prismatic batteries for Tesla ESS applications. SK On maintains a strategy focused on grid-scale ESS, primarily using pouch-type batteries.
 Samsung SDI's Malaysia plant serves as a key hub in the expansion of next-generation AI data centers. Established in 1991 as the company's first overseas subsidiary, the site has operated as a battery production base since 2012. Lee Jae-yong, chairman of Samsung Electronics, visited the facility in 2024 as his first official overseas trip after resolving legal issues, underscoring its strategic importance to the company's battery business.
